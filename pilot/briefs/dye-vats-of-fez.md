# Brief: dye-vats-of-fez ("The Dye Vats of Fez")

## 1. The world in one line
Late afternoon over the Chouara tannery in Fez el-Bali: you drop from a terrace into the honeycomb of round stone vats and ride the narrow stone ridges between them, while swallows skim the dye and drink on the wing. Each swallow takes the exact colour of the last vat it touched, and at the end you follow the last one down into the great indigo vat, where the portal waits on the bottom.
- **Twist**: the colour sticks. Each dip turns the whole bird the exact hex of that vat, so the flock becomes a moving palette of the tannery.
- **Keep**: id, name, registry caption; swallows as the subject; the half-second run of dips at the start (`TIMES`, lines 133-135); one palette shared by vats, splashes and birds (`DYES`, line 132); the splash rings, dye column and drops (lines 277-290); the great pit vat `HV` and the dive into it; the marbled under-surface (`pitC`, ink clouds, bubbles, lines 104-116 and 293-306); the portal at the vat bottom.
- **Fix** (from the sheet): at 0.5 s the frame is dark and the swallows are dark navy cut-outs. At 3-12 s the birds are flat paper triangles at 2.6x scale that fill a third of the frame. The vats are a few small cylinders beside a lane, not a honeycomb. The lower 40% is a flat sand plane with blurry blobs. The houses are plain boxes and the minaret is a box with a cone. At 15 s the frame is a blank pit wall and an ochre floor (a dead frame). At 18 s a large blown-out glow disc and speckle sit in the vat. A sun disc sits on the horizon from 1.5 to 12 s.

## 2. Focal subject
Candidates: (a) barn swallows (*Hirundo rustica*), which really do drink by skimming and dipping their bills on the wing; (b) the pallid and common swifts that scream over the medina; (c) the tannery's pigeons, whose droppings feed the lime vats; (d) the white storks that nest on Fez minarets; (e) the workers who tread the hides.
**Pick: (a), barn swallows, as accepted.** Their real drinking pass gives a beat for every dip: a shallow dive, a flare just above the surface, the bill touching, a spray, and a pull-up with fast wingbeats. Swifts (b) stay as a high far flock and pigeons (c) as supporting life. No human figures; workers are present only through their tools.
**Behaviour**: a pass is glide, then descent, then flare (the tail fans and the wings raise), then the touch (`T` = e.t), then 3-4 hard flaps up and a banked turn away. Between dips the birds weave in bursts of flapping and glides of 0.4-0.8 s, all on `t` so they are alive in the portal preview. A dyed bird trails 3-5 drops of its new colour for 0.5 s.
**The model** (the main upgrade): replace `mkSwallow` (lines 244-259) with a real cambered mesh from `birdGeometry` (src/kit/fauna/birds.js 37 onward), using the `swifts` proportions at line 26 with `fork: 1` and a longer tail (0.16) for streamers. Give it a lit material (Lambert or Standard, vertex colours baked white or grey) and set `material.color` to the vat hex (lerp over 0.25 s). Keep the belly about 35% lighter than the body and the bill dark. Set an emissive floor of 0.22 x colour so a bird is never black against the sky. Span 0.33 m x scale 1.8 to 2.0. Keep the birds 5 to 14 m from the camera: no bird closer than 4 m, and none covering more than about 1/8 of the frame. Start colour: the natural barn swallow (steel blue `#3a5aa8` back, rust throat `#c4623c`, cream belly). After the first dip the bird is fully dye-coloured, throat included, with a slightly darker back.

## 3. Beat list (19 s; `T = u*19`; nothing scheduled before T = 1.0)
- **0-2.5 s HOOK: the drop into the honeycomb.** Start 8 m up at the lip of a viewing terrace (`nearThrough` on), already moving, with a parapet of saffron hides sliding out of the lower frame. Ease down to a 2.0 m eye over the vat ridges by 2.4 s. Five swallows dive past and dip in a row of vats ahead at 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5 and 3.0 s (indigo, saffron, poppy, mint, rose). Each makes a coloured crown and ring, and each bird changes colour at once. *Top*: medina terraces and a minaret in warm light. *Middle*: the dipping birds. *Bottom*: the honeycomb filling with rings.
- **3-8 s The honeycomb run.** The camera follows a winding ridge path (S left). Dips slow to one every 0.8-1 s, and a bird that dips a second time changes to the new vat's colour. poi left (3.8-6 s) at the tannery wall: stained terraces, hides draped over the vat walls, wooden paddles, stacked buckets and a hose running down the plaster. *Top*: the tannery wall and balconies. *Middle*: 3 or 4 birds banking. *Bottom*: rows of vats with stained rims.
- **8-13 s The medina rises.** The camera lifts gently to 3.5 m and crosses the pale lime vats (white and grey, real), which make the colours pop by contrast. poi up-right (8.5-11 s) at Fez el-Bali: green-tiled roofs, two minarets, rooftops with saffron hides drying in rows, satellite dishes and laundry. At 10.5 s pigeons burst off a near roof and wheel. At 11.5 s a swallow dips in a henna vat and its ring spreads red-orange into the brown vat beside it, across a broken partition. *Top*: the skyline and minarets. *Middle*: the roofs with hides and pigeons. *Bottom*: the white vats with a red bloom.
- **13-16.5 s The last pass.** Ahead lies the great indigo vat (`HV`), bigger and deeper, with its rim stained in layers. All 8 swallows, each now a different colour, converge and dip in it in a quick ripple (one every 0.35 s, 13.6-16.0 s). Each comes up indigo, then they climb away against the ochre wall, with the swift flock high above. The camera descends toward the rim, staying 2.5 m or more above it. poi down at the surface (14.5-16.5 s). *Top*: the climbing indigo birds against ochre. *Middle and bottom*: the indigo surface laced with interfering rings.
- **16.5-19 s Into the vat.** The camera passes through the surface (keep `WATER` fog, made lighter: `#4a7ad8`, density 0.016). Marbled curtains and pigment clouds hold the frame; the last swallow's shadow and its ring are visible on the surface from below. The portal lies on the vat floor ahead and is entered at 19 s. There is no blank wall: aim the look down the vat centre, never at the pit wall.

## 4. Detail layers (target about 1,300 to 1,600 lines)
1. **Honeycomb of vats** (about 300 lines). 220 to 300 vats on a jittered hex grid with one relaxation pass, radii 0.75-1.4 m, so the packing is never a perfect grid. Shape each vat with a lathe profile whose top edge carries per-vertex noise (chips, a lower lip, an occasional broken notch). Fill the spaces between vats with one merged stone mass at rim height, with holes cut where the vats sit; these are the ridges people walk on. Give the rim texture layered tide-lines (a canvas atlas of 4 variants): bands of older dyes, drips running down, a lime-white crust and a dark wet band at the liquid line. Tint the stain toward each vat's own dye. Zones as at the real Chouara: white lime vats in one corner (about 25%), coloured vats in the middle, brown tannin vats at the edges. Set each liquid level 0.15-0.5 m below the rim. Liquid is a shader with a slow marble (reuse `dyeT`), a fresnel sheen of the sky and a sun glint capped at 0.8. About 30 vats hold a half-sunk pale hide.
2. **Lower frame: the ridges and their clutter** (about 150 lines). Wet stone tops, darker in their middle, with dyed footprints and puddles. About 90 hides draped over vat walls: a plane bent over the rim, with sag and curled corners, coloured cream, ochre or saffron. Wooden paddles leaning on rims, stacked wooden and plastic buckets, coiled hose, stained rags and a few sacks of pigment. Everything scattered, rotated and tinted per instance.
3. **Tannery terraces, 15-45 m** (about 250 lines). A ring of 2 to 4 storey buildings around the pit, never closer than 15 m to the line. Stepped walls in ochre, rose and lime white, stained at the base. Wooden balconies with turned rails (the viewing terraces), arched doors, iron grilles and drainpipes. Parapets heaped with drying hides, and flat roofs with hides laid in irregular rows (instanced, each with its own colour, curl and rotation). Use per-instance tint like towers-over-the-old-city.js 1640-1662 (one material, tint and tone by house).
4. **Medina beyond, 45-500 m** (about 250 lines). 400 to 600 stacked flat-roof houses on two rising slopes, each with parapets, water tanks, satellite dishes (dish plus arm, about 120), laundry lines with 3-6 cloths, and a few rooftop shacks. Add green glazed tile roofs: 6 to 10 hipped roofs with ridges (zawiyas and mosques). Build 3 minarets properly: a square shaft with a zellige band of green and white panels, blind arches, a crenellated top, a small lantern with a green tile cap and the three gold balls of the jamour. Replace the box and cone at lines 219-222. Beyond: the hills of Fez with the Merinid tombs as a pale ruin on the north ridge. Put `fx.makeMist` banks between the near medina, the far medina and the hills (src/kit/fx.js 1265), warm ochre at the bottom and blue at the top.
5. **Life** (about 250 lines). The 8 hero swallows (section 2). A high swift flock: `makeFlockAsync` with `swifts` and 30 birds, about 40 m up, with `keepClear` (copy azulejo-descent.js 1032-1046, including the `glaze` colour object and `clearOf`). Pigeons: 2 or 3 grey flocks on roofs, with one burst at 10.5 s (the pigeon rings at towers-over-the-old-city.js 2416-2440, `crows` proportions tinted slate grey).
6. **Splashes** (about 150 lines). Keep the rings, the column and the drops. Add: the ring colour is the vat dye a little lighter, and it leaves a spreading lighter "bloom" that marbles back over 4 s. Drops are small lit spheres in the dye colour, not glow points. Add a crown of 8-12 short petals for 0.25 s, and a trail of 3-5 drops behind the bird. Where the bloom meets a broken partition it bleeds into the next vat.
7. **Sky and light** (about 60 lines). Late afternoon. Keep `makeSky`, with a pale warm horizon and a clear blue zenith. The sun sits behind the camera and to the left (around azimuth 150° from the flight direction, elevation 20°), so walls ahead take a raking ochre light and swallows are lit side-on. **Delete `sunM` (lines 238-241)**: no sun disc. Use a hemisphere light (sky `#9db8d8` over ground `#c9a070`) so shadow sides stay warm.
8. **Under the surface** (about 100 lines). Keep it, lighter: caustic ripples on the curtains, the pigment clouds 30% smaller, and bubbles as tiny points (size ≤0.12).

## 5. Camera path and pois
Fly at constant speed, about 5 m/s over roughly 95 m. Start 8 m up at the terrace lip and ease down to 2.0 m above the ridges by 2.4 s. Follow an S along the ridges (left, then right), rise to 3.5 m from 8 to 13 s, then descend 4.5 to 2.6 m to the `HV` rim and plunge in. Always forward, with a step bob ≤0.03. The camera stands on the ridge stone at all times. Corridor: no wall, balcony, tower or hanging hide within 15 m, and no bird within 4 m. Clear the first 20 m of the terrace lip below the start except for the parapet hides, kept 3 m or more under the eye.
pois: the tannery wall (L, 3.8-6 s), the skyline (R and up, 8.5-11 s), the pigeons' roof (R, 10.5-12 s), the indigo surface (down, 14.5-16.5 s). Fade ≥0.3, weight ≤0.85.

## 6. Light and palette
Bloom strength 0.3, threshold 0.95, radius 0.45; exposure 0.85. Vats: indigo `#2f5fd0`, saffron `#f0b030`, poppy `#d4552f`, mint `#3fbf8a`, rose `#e0709a`, henna `#c0602a`, lime white `#e8e4d8`, tannin brown `#8a6a3a`. Walls: ochre `#c9a37a` and `#d8b080`. Tiles: `#2f7a5a`. Sky: blue to warm. Mean frame brightness 0.25 or more throughout, and ≥0.12 under the surface. Shader colours through pow(col, 2.2). No flicker; sheen moves slowly.

## 7. Portal
A small pale ring lying on the floor of the great indigo vat, colour-matched to saffron so it reads against the blue. The vat limits its size.
`{ shape:'circle', style:'ring', radius:3.4, color:'#ffcf8a', veil:'#ffcf8a', glow:0.6, halo:true, revealDistance:700 }`, with `portal.style` explicit. Keep it about 9 m past the surface entry point. Its halo must stay within about 1.5x the ring: no blown disc and no speckle around it (the 18 s fault).

## 8. Risks (what a judge will fail first)
1. **The swallows.** Flat, dark or too big. Check 3, 6 and 9 s: cambered, lit bodies, colours that match their vat exactly, no bird over 1/8 of the frame, and flapping in bursts, not constantly.
2. **A grid, not a honeycomb.** Vats in rows of identical cylinders, or clean rims. Packing, chips and layered stains must read at 1.5 s.
3. **Dead frames and glare** at 15-18 s: a blank pit wall, a dark vat interior, or the portal halo blooming. Also any sun disc on the horizon.
