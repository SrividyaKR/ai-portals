# Brief: dragon-blood-forest ("The Dragon Blood Forest")

## 1. The world in one line
Dusk on the Dixam limestone plateau of Socotra: you drop out of a sea of dragon blood umbrellas, follow Socotra starlings down a faint mossy trail under the trees, and the rising pink moon wakes the forest, sending a wave of peach light along every rosette tip toward Haggeher's peaks and the moon.

- **Twist**: the moon wakes the forest. A soft peach wave runs ahead of the camera, lighting the tips of the needle rosettes all the way to the moon. Nothing else is fantasy.
- **This is the owner's favourite, accepted as "Is Complete. I would use this."** We are DEEPENING it (448 lines now, aim for about 1,200 to 1,400), not changing it. It runs first in the tour with **seed 7**, so check every frame at seed 7.
- **Keep exactly**: the dusk-pink sky (`makeSky` colours at lines 44-50, fog `#c07c9c` 0.0029) with dark layered silhouettes; the real branching trees (`makeVariant`/`grow`/`rosette`, lines 105-156) with their spiky needle rosettes; the faint glow on the leaves "as if in mild bloom"; the big pink moon (lines 386-392) as the portal's landmark; the faint mossy trail; birds as the guide; **the wake wave** (lines 207-239; its front leaves at 6.5 s, line 403) as THE moment; the opening over the canopy sea and the descent through the clearing `GAP`; the path `pts` and `duration = 17`; **the tree clearance rule `ax < 90 && jz < 170` with `clr = 3 + 5.2*sh` (lines 173-177), unchanged**: an earlier edit that cleared more trees made the world barren. Do not thin the forest.
- **Fix** (from the sheet): (a) from 3 s on, the bottom 40% of the frame is a flat mauve plane, and the trail is a 3-vertex strip that reads as a blurry teal-green glowing smear (a "runway"); `groundTex` is built but never used (the ground material is `vertexColors` only). (b) At 12 and 15 s the birds are large rigid black chevrons, too near and too few, with no species. (c) Mid-distance between the trees is empty ground: no understorey, no Socotra plants beyond the desert roses. (d) The far layers are three flat ridge strips with no mist between them. (e) At 15 s the ring and its halo already take about a quarter of the frame width.

## 2. Focal subject
Candidates: (a) **Socotra starling** *Onychognathus frater* (endemic, glossy blue-black, with **rufous-orange primaries that flash in flight**, flocks that move between crowns and come down to wadi pools); (b) Egyptian vulture (Socotra has the densest population on Earth, white bodies and black flight feathers, wheeling); (c) Socotra sunbird; (d) Socotra buzzard; (e) the resin itself (the trunks "bleeding").
**Pick: (a) Socotra starlings as the guide flock** (this keeps the owner's "birds as a guide"). They are native, dark against a pink sky (the owner's silhouette look), and the orange wing flash gives each wingbeat colour. Real behaviour: they fly in bursts of quick flaps between glides, **land on a crown, then all lift off at once**, swoop low over a wadi pool to drink, and rise again. That gives beats; a lerp along the path does not. **Supporting**: 3 to 5 **Egyptian vultures** wheeling slowly high against the moon and the ridges (far layer, never near).

Enter at 17 s; no glide after it.
